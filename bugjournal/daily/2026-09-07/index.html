Learnings#

Key Learnings#

  • Successful compilation for mixed-precision (float islands) on HTP does not guarantee 4-bit execution; it often triggers a silent fallback to float for entire operator chains.
  • For partially observable games, a simulation that models ‘cost-to-uncover’ is significantly more effective than pure RL or greedy heuristics.
  • Enforcement is more effective when implemented at the engine/logic level (setStatus/applyApproval) rather than just the UI level.
  • Strictly enforcing an ‘idea-first’ workflow via AI guardrails prevents uncontrolled code sprawl but requires the AI to be highly aware of the ‘status’ and ‘doing’ states of the graph.
  • The distinction between ‘clean’ and ‘dirty’ memory is vital for interpreting peak memory metrics: weights are file-mapped clean pages and do not contribute to the non-clean ‘memory footprint’.
  • In neural networks, the step function’s lack of gradient is critical because the derivative is zero almost everywhere, not just at the threshold.
  • Multi-session coordination via ‘coord’ is available but not automatically used by agents; manual coordination is required to prevent simultaneous graph writes.
  • The importance of ‘container ideas’ in a large-scale project to prevent cognitive overload and maintain a manageable graph hierarchy.
