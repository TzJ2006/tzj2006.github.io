General Limitations#

  • The package setup check initially failed to catch the macOS SciPy incompatibility during the standard verification flow.
  • The AI’s safety classifier blocked legitimate production deployment commands to the remote server, requiring manual user intervention.
  • Scanning was unable to inspect contents inside non-text binary members of compressed archives (e.g., npz, hdf5).

  • The agent cannot physically ‘see’ the HTML output visually. Instead, it implemented a custom ‘check_html.py’ script to parse the DOM and verify that all figures were correctly linked, anchors were unbroken, and technical placeholders were empty.
  • Route tasks (PatternLock, RouteStick) use a legacy Gemini-based implementation and do not follow the new harness interpreter logic.
  • The ‘one-call-per-episode’ architecture is the target/goal, not yet the proven result in the current stage.
  • Access to tianhe3 is subject to permission classifier ‘Shared Cluster Mutation’. I must use setsid nohup carefully to avoid triggering blocks.
  • AI cannot validate clinical ’truth’ of a retrieval; it only measures similarity in the learned latent space relative to the query.

Learnings#

Key Learnings#

  • Contact-heavy robotic tasks (like coffee pod insertion) are highly sensitive to cross-platform floating-point differences, causing exponential divergence even with identical starting states.
  • Constraining VLM behavior (moving from ‘reporting coordinates’ to ‘making selections’) significantly improves task success rates in robotic manipulation.
  • Git history remains a major leak vector even after files are removed from the working directory; .gitignore only prevents future commits.
  • Even in controlled environments, identical noise seeds do not guarantee bit-identical rollouts due to floating-point non-determinism in GPU operations (confirmed by the ‘dupagree’ check).
  • The ‘counting’ success rate might be lower with the harness because the bottleneck lies in the policy’s action response, not the memory accuracy (as seen in the discrepancy between Oracle and Harness).

Practical Learnings#

  • WeChat has a limit of approximately 999 collected emoticons and does not natively support WebP for emoticons.
  • A patch-size mismatch (504 vs 256) in image models must be explicitly documented in the notebook metadata to prevent confusion with standard training protocols.
  • Small differences in image interpolation/resizing can significantly shift AUROC for low-margin tasks like ‘holding’ (PickXtimes).
  • Visual prompts on its own don’t replace text, but enable policy usage without structural changes/longer inputs.
